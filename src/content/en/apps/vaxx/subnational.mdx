# Sub-national analysis

The **Sub-National Analysis** section repeats the national analysis for regions (Admin 1) and districts (Admin 2). It has three pages: **Sub-National Coverage**, **Sub-National Inequality** and **Coverage Target**. Averages at national level often hide weak regions and districts, so this is where programme action is usually targeted. Methods: **[sub-national analysis](/en/docs/framework/7-subnational-analysis)**.

These pages need adjusted data and use the vaccination denominator chosen in **[Denominators](/en/apps/vaxx/denominators)**. Their indicator tabs and the Custom Check tab work as described in **[Coverage, equity and targets](/en/apps/vaxx/coverage-equity-targets)**.

## Sub-National Coverage

Compares DHIS2 and survey estimates of coverage in each region.

<Steps>

### Set the geography

Use **Admin Level** (Admin 1 or Admin 2) and the region selector. Choose Admin 1 with no region to see regions, or pick a region to see its districts. At Admin 2 the selector can also pick a district.

### Pick an indicator

Choose a tab, or **Custom Check** for any Vaxx indicator, including dropout, zero-dose and under-vaccinated.

### Read the chart

The chart is titled "... Coverage Estimates" for the selected area and shows the DHIS2 estimate, with the WUENIC and survey estimates where they exist for that geography. The caption gives the denominator.

</Steps>

![Sub-national coverage](/images/subnational-coverage-targets.png)

Survey and WUENIC estimates appear only where they exist for the selected geography, so some charts show DHIS2 alone.

## Sub-National Inequality

Shows how coverage differs between units inside one geography.

- Filters: **Admin Level** and region. Each chart plots the coverage of every sub-national unit by year (legend "Coverage at subnational unit") against the coverage of the selected region or the country (legend "National Coverage", or the region name followed by "Coverage").
- The subtitle says which units are shown: "Subnational unit: admin 1 level", "district level" or the region name.
- Tabs and the Custom Check tab work as on the national page.

Use it to find districts that sit far below their region, and to see whether the gap narrows over time. A district that is always low is different from one that is low only in a bad year: the second case may be a data problem.

## Coverage Target

The share of districts in a region meeting the global targets.

1. Choose the **region** (this page has no admin level filter: the unit is always the district).
2. Read the two tabs. **Vaccine** covers BCG, Pentavalent 3 and Measles 1 (coverage at least 80 percent at district level). **Dropout** covers Penta 1 to Penta 3 and Penta 3 to Measles 1 (dropout below 10 percent).
3. The chart title, "Percentage of districts in (region name) meeting the ... target", shows the threshold used. Bars are percentages by year and indicator.
4. The table lists the districts meeting the target for the indicator and year you select. Download it as Excel with the table's download button.

## Interpretation and pitfalls

<Callout type="warning">
District denominators are the least reliable. A district with coverage over 100 percent, or one that flips between years, is more likely to have a population or reporting problem than a real change. Cross-check it in **[Data quality](/en/apps/vaxx/data-quality)**.
</Callout>

- Small districts give noisy percentages. Read several years, not one.
- Do not compare Admin 1 and Admin 2 charts directly: units differ in size and reliability.
- Changing the admin level resets the region selection.
