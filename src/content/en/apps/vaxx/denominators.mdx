# Denominators

Coverage is a number of vaccinated children divided by a target population, so the choice of denominator matters as much as the data quality. This section has two pages under **Denominators**: **Population Trend** (the Denominator Assessment page) and **Denominator Selection**. Both need adjusted data. The method is in the **[denominator selection](/en/docs/framework/4-denominator-selection)** chapter.

## When to use it

Use these pages after adjustment and before reading any coverage result. Repeat them if you change the adjustment or the survey inputs.

## Population Trend

Compares population trends across the available sources.

- Filter **Select Best Population**: **Live births (DHIS2)**, **Total births (DHIS2)**, **Total population (DHIS2)** or **Under 1 year (DHIS2)**. It sets the DHIS2 population the derived denominators are built from.
- Card **Population Comparison** with tabs **Population**, **Births** and **Under 1**. Each chart plots the UN projection against the DHIS2 projection by year (in thousands).

Look for a stable, plausible gap between UN and DHIS2. A sudden break in one line usually means a change in how the population was projected, not a real change in births.

![Population trend](/images/denominator-population-trend.png)

## Denominator Selection

Compares coverage under each denominator option and records your choice.

<Steps>

### Read the survey comparison

The **National Coverage** card has tabs **Institutional Live Births**, **BCG**, **Pentavalent 3** and **Measles 1**. Each chart shows the facility-based coverage from DHIS2 under each denominator, next to the national survey coverage for the survey year. A denominator that gives coverage close to the survey, without exceeding 100 percent, is a good candidate.

If survey estimates are missing, the card shows "No coverage to show yet" and a **Go to Load Data** button. Add the survey values in the National Rates step.

### Check the regional pattern

**Subnational Derived Coverage** has tabs **Pentavalent 1**, **Pentavalent 3** and **Measles 1**. It compares regional estimates by denominator for the survey year.

### Check the trend

**Population Trend** (on this page it means coverage over time) has tabs **Pentavalent 1**, **Pentavalent 3** and **Measles 1**. Each shows coverage by year under every denominator, including the UN population. Use the **Admin Level** filter (National, Admin 1, Admin 2) and the region selector to look at one area.

### Choose the denominator

Set the **Vaccination denominator** chip. Options are **DHIS2**, **ANC 1**, **Pentavalent 1**, **Penta1-derived** and **ANC 1 Population Growth**. The choice is saved in the dataset and used by every coverage, inequality and target page. The Vaxx App has one vaccination denominator, with no separate maternal one.

</Steps>

![Best denominator selection](/images/denominator-best-selection.png)

![National coverage over time by denominator](/images/denominator-national-coverage-overtime.png)

## What each option means

| Option | Idea |
| --- | --- |
| DHIS2 | The population projected in the DHIS2 data (live births, total births, total population or under 1, per the Select Best Population setting). |
| ANC 1 | Denominator derived from ANC-1 service utilization. |
| Pentavalent 1 | Denominator derived from Penta-1 immunization. |
| ANC 1 Population Growth, Penta1-derived | The ANC-1 or Penta-1 derivation with an adjustment for annual population growth. |

The UN projection is also plotted on the charts for comparison, but it is not one of the choices in the chip.

Definitions of derived populations and survey rates are in the **[framework chapter](/en/docs/framework/4-denominator-selection)**.

## Interpretation and pitfalls

<Callout type="warning">
A denominator derived from Penta1 makes Penta1 coverage look near 100 percent by construction. Judge it on Penta3 and Measles 1, and against the survey.
</Callout>

- Do not pick the option that gives the highest coverage. Pick the one that best agrees with the survey and stays consistent over time.
- Sub-national denominators can be less reliable than national ones. Read them together with district data quality.
- The vaccination denominator label appears as a chip on every analysis page. If results look odd, check it first.
