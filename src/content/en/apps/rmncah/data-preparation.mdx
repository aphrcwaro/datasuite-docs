# Data preparation

Data preparation turns the reported numbers into an analysis-ready dataset. It has three pages in the **Data quality** section: **Remove Years**, **Data Adjustment** and **Data Adjustment Changes**. Running the adjustment is what unlocks the Denominators and Analysis pages. The method, formulas and business rules are in [Data adjustment](/en/docs/framework/3-data-adjustment).

## Remove Years

**Purpose.** Leave out years whose data cannot be relied on, for example a year with almost no reporting or a system-wide failure.

**When to use it.** After the [data quality](/en/apps/rmncah/data-quality) pages show a year that is clearly erratic and you have a real-world reason to believe it. If you have no reason, keep the year.

<Steps>

### Open Remove Years

It is under **Data quality** in the sidebar.

### Select Years to Remove

The chips list the years present in the data. Select one or more.

### Press Confirm Removal

The message changes from *No years have been removed yet* to *Data for the following years has been removed*, followed by the years. To bring a year back, deselect it and confirm again.

</Steps>

![Remove years](/images/dqa-remove-year.png)

## Data Adjustment

**Purpose.** Correct for incomplete reporting and extreme outliers. Missing and outlier values are replaced using the district median for the same year. Reported counts are then scaled up with a K-factor, which represents the volume of services in non-reporting facilities relative to reporting ones.

<Steps>

### Choose the K-Factors

The card **K-Factors (Adjustment Factors)** has four selectors: **ANC K-Factor**, **Delivery K-Factor**, **Vaccines K-Factor** and **OPD K-Factor**. Each offers 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1. A value of 0 assumes that non-reporting facilities delivered no services, 0.25 assumes a quarter of the volume of reporting facilities, and 1 the same volume. Raise a factor when facilities that report poorly, such as private ones, provide a large share of that service.

### Press Adjust Data

The message goes from *Dataset not adjusted* to *Adjusting ...* and then *Data Adjusted*. Do this even if you keep every factor at 0, because it is what unlocks the analysis pages.

### Download the result if you need it

**Download Adjusted Dataset** saves the adjusted master dataset as a `.dta` file.

</Steps>

<Callout type="warning">
Changing the K-factors or the removed years discards the adjusted dataset. Press **Adjust Data** again, otherwise the analysis pages will not reflect your change. If the adjustment fails, the message shows the reason.
</Callout>

According to the methodology, no adjustment is applied for districts whose reporting rate is below 75%, because imputation is unreliable there. The methodology flags such years as unreliable for coverage calculations.

![K-factor selection](/images/adjustment-kfactor.png)

## Data Adjustment Changes

**Purpose.** See what the adjustment did to each indicator.

**How to read it.** The card **Visualize effects of changes** has one tab per indicator: Institutional Deliveries, Institutional Live Births, Pentavalent 1, ANC 1 and OPD Under 5 Years, plus a **Custom** tab for any other indicator. Each chart compares the number of events *before* and *after* adjustment for completeness and outliers, by year. Use **Download Plot** and **Download Data** on the toolbar. **Generate Report** opens the adjustment report.

![Before and after adjustment](/images/adjustment-before-after.png)

## How to interpret the results

- A small gap between the lines means reporting was already nearly complete.
- A wide gap in one year points to a year with low reporting. Report the K-factor used and the percentage change it produced, as the methodology recommends.
- The effect is largest for services with the lowest reporting rates. Compare with the [Reporting Rate](/en/apps/rmncah/data-quality) page.

## Common pitfalls

- Removing a year you should keep silently shortens every later trend.
- Using a high K-factor to hide poor reporting hides the problem instead of fixing it. Document your choice.
- Mortality indicators are not adjusted for reporting or outliers, because deaths are few and volatile (see [Mortality](/en/apps/rmncah/mortality)).
