# Sub-national analysis

National averages hide the regions and districts that are being left behind. The **Sub-National Analysis** group has three pages: **Sub-National Coverage**, **Sub-National Inequality** and **Coverage Target**. They use the same adjusted data and the same denominators as the national pages (see [Denominators](/en/apps/rmncah/denominators)). The method is described in [Subnational analysis](/en/docs/framework/7-subnational-analysis).

<Callout type="info">
Expect more noise at district level than at region level. Small numbers, patient movement between districts and reporting gaps all produce implausibly high or low values.
</Callout>

## Sub-National Coverage

**Purpose.** Compare DHIS2 and survey coverage estimates for each region or district.

**When to use it.** After National Coverage, to find the areas behind the national figure.

<Steps>

### Open Sub-National Coverage

It is in the **Sub-National Analysis** group.

### Set the geography

Choose the **Admin Level**. With **Admin 1** a region selector appears, and with **Admin 2** a district selector, so that you can focus on one area.

### Pick an indicator tab

**ANC 4 Visits**, **Institutional Live Births**, **Low Birth Weight**, **Pentavalent 3**, **Measles 1**, **FP Projection** or **Custom** for any other indicator.

### Read the chart

The title names the area, for example the coverage estimates of the selected region. Lines show the DHIS2 estimate and the survey estimate, and the caption names the denominator used. **Download Plot** and **Download Data** are in the toolbar.

</Steps>

![Sub-national coverage](/images/Subnational_coverage.png)

**How to interpret.** Compare the region with the national line. A survey point far from the DHIS2 line in one region often signals a denominator or reporting problem there. Check the [Reporting Rate](/en/apps/rmncah/data-quality) of the region before drawing conclusions.

## Sub-National Inequality

**Purpose.** Show how far each region (or each district inside a region) is from the national or regional figure, year by year.

**Filters.** **Admin Level** and, at Admin 1, a region selector to zoom in on one region.

Tabs are **ANC 4 Visits**, **Institutional Live Births**, **Low Birth Weight**, **Pentavalent 3**, **Measles 1** and **Custom**. Each dot is a sub-national unit (**Coverage at subnational unit**) and the line is the national or, when a region is chosen, the regional coverage. A spread that narrows over the years means inequality is falling. The methodology summarises this spread with the median absolute deviation from the median (MADM).

![Sub-national inequality](/images/subnational-coverage-inequality.png)

## Coverage Target

**Purpose.** Show the percentage of districts in a region that meet a coverage target, by year.

**Filter.** A region selector. The level is fixed to districts.

Tabs are **ANC 4 Visits**, **Institutional Live Births** and **Vaccine**. The targets are the same as on the national page: at least 70% for ANC4, at least 80% for institutional live births and vaccines, and dropout below 10%. A table under the chart lists the districts that reach the target for the indicator chosen in its *This table only* control.

The goal is that all districts meet the target, so a higher percentage means less inequality.

![Sub-national coverage against targets](/images/subnational-coverage-targets.png)

## Common pitfalls

- Reading a single district year in isolation. Look at the trend and the neighbouring years.
- Comparing regions that have very different populations without checking numerators.
- Forgetting that the denominator selected for the whole country also drives regional results. If one region looks impossible (above 100%), test another denominator before flagging the region.
- Ranking areas on the latest year only. Combine level, change over time, reporting completeness and denominator quality, as the [methodology](/en/docs/framework/5-coverage-estimation) suggests.
