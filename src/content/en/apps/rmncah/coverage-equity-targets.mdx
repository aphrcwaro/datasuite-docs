# Coverage, equity and targets

These four pages sit in the **National Analysis** group of the sidebar: **National Coverage**, **Coverage Target**, **National Inequality** (routine data) and **Equity Assessment** (survey data). All of them need Data Adjustment to have been run and use the denominators chosen on [Denominator Selection](/en/apps/rmncah/denominators). Methods are in [National coverage](/en/docs/framework/5-coverage-estimation) and [Equity assessment](/en/docs/framework/6-equity-analysis).

## National Coverage

**Purpose.** Compare facility-based coverage with survey and WUENIC estimates for each indicator.

**When to use it.** After denominators are chosen, to judge whether facility trends are believable.

<Steps>

### Open National Coverage

There are no filters. The page uses the national level and the denominators you selected.

### Pick an indicator tab

Tabs: **ANC 4 Visits**, **Institutional Live Births**, **Low Birth Weight**, **Pentavalent 3**, **Measles 1**, **FP Projection** (see [Advanced analysis](/en/apps/rmncah/advanced-analysis)) and **Custom** for any other indicator.

### Read the chart

The lines are the **DHIS2 estimate**, the **WUENIC estimate** and the **Survey estimate** with its **95% CI**, from 2010. The caption names the denominator that was used. Use **Download Plot** or **Download Data** in the toolbar.

</Steps>

![National coverage](/images/national-coverage.png)

**How to interpret.** If facility and survey values are close, confidence in the trend is higher. Facility values far above the survey suggest a denominator problem or over-reporting. Values far below suggest incomplete reporting, missing private-sector data or missing outreach. Coverage above 100% is a data quality signal, not good performance.

## Coverage Target

**Purpose.** Show the percentage of regions or districts that reach a coverage target, by year.

**Filter.** **Admin Level** (Admin 1 or Admin 2).

Tabs are **ANC 4 Visits**, **Institutional Live Births** and **Vaccine** (BCG, Pentavalent 3, Measles 1 and the dropout rates). The chart title states the rule, for example the percentage of units meeting the target. The thresholds applied to regions and districts are:

| Indicator | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| ANC4 | Coverage of at least 70% |
| Institutional live births | Coverage of at least 80% |
| Vaccines | Coverage of at least 80% |
| Vaccine dropout | Dropout below 10% |

Below the chart a table, **Target Rate by Region** (Admin 1) or **Sub-National with coverage >90%** (Admin 2), lists the units that reach the target for the indicator you choose in the *This table only* control. Its title mentions 90%, but the calculation applies the thresholds above. Higher percentages mean less geographic inequality. The same page for regions and districts is described under [Sub-national analysis](/en/apps/rmncah/subnational).

![Coverage against targets](/images/national-coverage-targets.png)

## National Inequality (routine data)

**Purpose.** See how much regions differ from the national figure, using facility data.

The card **National Inequality** has one tab per default indicator (ANC 4 Visits, Institutional Live Births, Low Birth Weight, Pentavalent 3, Measles 1) plus **Custom**. Each dot is an Admin 1 region; the line is **National Coverage**. A wide spread of dots means high inequality.

Under it, choose **Select Years** and a **Palette** (Greens, Blues, Reds or Purples) for the **Sub-National Mapping** card, which maps the coverage of each region for the selected years.

![National inequality](/images/national-coverage-inequality.png)

## Equity Assessment (survey data)

**Purpose.** Show inequality between population groups, using household survey data.

**When to use it.** When survey files were loaded (or the built-in ones cover your country) and you want to know who is being left behind.

<Steps>

### Choose the Equity Type

**Area** (Rural, Urban), **Maternal Education** (No education, Primary, Secondary or higher) or **Wealth Quintile**.

### Pick an indicator tab

ANC 4 Visits, Institutional Live Births, Low Birth Weight, Pentavalent 3, Measles 1 or **Custom**.

### Read the equiplot

Each group has its own line over time, with the national trend for comparison. **Generate Report** opens the national inequality report.

</Steps>

For wealth, read the pattern: only the richest ahead means top inequality, evenly spaced groups mean linear inequality, and the poorest far behind means bottom inequality (marginalisation). Each calls for a different programme response. Focus on the latest data point, the change since 2010 and whether the gap is closing.

![Equity by area of residence](/images/national-coverage-equity-area.png)

![Equity by wealth quintile](/images/national-coverage-wealth.png)

![Equity by maternal education](/images/national-coverage-education.png)

## Common pitfalls

- Survey points are few and have confidence intervals. Do not over-read small differences.
- Facility inequality between regions can come from denominator or reporting differences, not real differences in access.
- The equity pages rely on the survey files and the survey year from Load Data.
